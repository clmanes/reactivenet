# Data processing agreement

**A contract template.** This is not an information page: it is the text to be
signed by the parties, and it is annexed to the service contract. Before using
it, check case by case **whether a processor relationship genuinely arises**: in
ordinary use of the application, data does not leave the client's browser and
the provider processes nothing on its behalf — in which case this agreement is
not needed, and signing it would describe a state of affairs that does not
exist.

It is needed where the client entrusts the provider with actual processing: use
of **shared spaces** hosted by the provider, use of **short links** hosted by
the provider, or **support** activities involving access to the client's data.

### Art. 1 — Subject matter, duration, nature and purpose

The Controller appoints the Processor under Article 28 GDPR for the processing
described in Annex A, for the duration of the Contract only and for the purposes
stated there alone. On termination of the Contract, Article 9 applies.

### Art. 2 — Documented instructions

The Processor processes personal data **only on documented instructions** from
the Controller, including as regards transfers to third countries. The Contract
and this agreement, with their annexes, constitute the initial and complete
instructions; further instructions are given in writing to the addresses in
Article 12.

The Processor immediately informs the Controller if, in its opinion, an
instruction infringes the GDPR or other data protection provisions, and may
suspend performance of the disputed instruction until the matter is clarified.

The Processor does not process the data for its own purposes, does not disclose
it to third parties, and does not use it to train models or for statistical
work unrelated to the Contract.

Where the Processor is required to process data by Union or Member State law, it
informs the Controller before doing so, unless that law prohibits such
information on important grounds of public interest.

### Art. 3 — Confidentiality

The Processor ensures that the persons authorised to process the data — here,
the Processor himself, as a natural person, and any external collaborators he
engages, expressly authorised — have committed themselves to confidentiality or
are under an appropriate statutory obligation of confidentiality, and are
instructed under Article 29 GDPR. The list of authorised persons is provided to
the Controller on request.

### Art. 4 — Security measures

The Processor implements the appropriate technical and organisational measures
under Article 32 GDPR, described in Annex B, and keeps them up to date. Measures
may be changed provided the level of security is not reduced; substantial
changes are notified to the Controller.

### Art. 5 — Sub-processors

The Controller gives **general authorisation** for the use of the sub-processors
listed in **Annex C**.

The Processor informs the Controller of any intention to add or replace a
sub-processor with at least **30 days'** notice; within that period the
Controller may object on reasonable grounds relating to data protection. In the
event of an objection the Parties shall seek an alternative in good faith; if
none is possible, either Party may terminate the affected part of the Contract
without penalty.

Sub-processors are bound by contract to **the same obligations** as those set
out in this agreement. The Processor remains liable to the Controller for its
sub-processors' failures.

### Art. 6 — Assistance to the Controller

The Processor, taking into account the nature of the processing and the
information available to it:

a) assists the Controller, through appropriate technical and organisational
measures, in responding to requests to exercise data subjects' rights
(Articles 15–22 GDPR), forwarding without delay — and in any event within
**5 working days** — any request received directly, without responding to it
itself;

b) assists the Controller in ensuring compliance with Articles 32–36 GDPR,
including data protection impact assessments and prior consultation;

c) makes available to the Controller all information necessary to demonstrate
compliance with the obligations of Article 28 GDPR.

**A technical limit to be understood before contracting.** Content synchronised
in shared spaces and documents behind short links are **end-to-end encrypted**:
the Processor holds ciphertext and does not hold the keys. It is therefore
unable to read, extract, rectify or export that content, or to respond on the
merits to an access or portability request concerning it; it can delete it, and
does so on the Controller's instruction. Reading, rectification and export are
available to the Controller through the application's own features. This is the
effect of the security measure chosen, and it is why it was chosen.

### Art. 7 — Personal data breaches

The Processor informs the Controller **without undue delay and in any event
within 24 hours** of becoming aware of a personal data breach affecting data
processed on the Controller's behalf, providing: the nature of the breach, the
categories and approximate number of data subjects and records concerned, the
likely consequences, the measures taken or proposed, and a contact point for
further information. It gives the Controller the cooperation needed to comply
with Articles 33 and 34 GDPR. Notification to the supervisory authority and
communication to data subjects remain the Controller's responsibility.

### Art. 8 — Audits

The Processor allows the Controller, or an auditor mandated by it and bound by
confidentiality, to carry out **audits and inspections** of compliance with this
agreement, on at least **15 days'** written notice, during working hours,
without prejudice to service continuity and to the confidentiality of other
clients' data. Save where an audit is required by the authorities or follows a
breach, the Controller may exercise this right once a year; further audits are
at the Controller's expense.

The Processor may discharge the obligations of this Article in the first
instance by providing documentation, certifications or the results of audits
already available.

### Art. 9 — Termination

On termination of the Contract, at the Controller's written choice, the
Processor **deletes or returns** the personal data processed on its behalf and
deletes existing copies, within **30 days**, unless retention is required by
Union or Member State law — in which case the data is kept only for the time and
purpose so required. Absent any indication within 30 days of termination, the
Processor proceeds to deletion. On request it issues written confirmation that
deletion has taken place.

### Art. 10 — Transfers outside the EU

The Processor does not transfer data outside the European Economic Area except
to the sub-processors listed in Annex C and where an adequacy decision or the
standard contractual clauses under Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/914 are in
place, together with the supplementary measures resulting from the assessment of
the specific case.

### Art. 11 — Liability and relationship with the Contract

Each Party is liable under Article 82 GDPR. As between the Parties, the
liability caps agreed in the Contract also apply to this agreement, save where
void by law and provided that they do not operate against data subjects or
supervisory authorities.

In the event of conflict between this agreement and the Contract, **this
agreement prevails** as regards the processing of personal data.

## Annex B — Technical and organisational measures (Art. 32)

The measures adopted are described in the Processor's «Security policy», which
forms an integral part of this agreement. In summary:

- **Structural minimisation**: in ordinary use of the application, data does not
  leave the user's device; the Processor holds no central store of content.
- **End-to-end encryption** of synchronised content and of documents shared via
  short link; keys are never held by the Processor.
- **Encryption in transit** (TLS, HSTS) across all services.
- **Role-based access control**, enforced server-side; passwords stored as
  hashes.
- **Layered application defence against cross-site scripting**: mandatory HTML
  sanitisation, values inserted as text, a Content Security Policy with no
  `unsafe-inline` or `unsafe-eval` for scripts, Trusted Types.
- **Isolation of document code execution** in a worker with a time limit.
- **Administrative access** restricted to the Processor in person, with
  multi-factor authentication where available.
- **Dependency updates** and automated security tests that fail the build on a
  policy regression.
- **Breach register** and the notification procedure in Article 7.
- **Availability**: user data stays on the device and can be exported as
  Markdown and CSV; responsibility for backing up its own content remains with
  the Controller.
